Photo Album

Plane Radar

    
The device fetches ADS-B positions itself over HTTPS, once per
      poll, and only while the radar is the screen on show. Nothing runs on your PC.


    

      
      
    

    
Your own position, in decimal degrees. In Google Maps, right-click
      where you are and the first entry on the menu is the pair, ready to paste.


    

      
Background Image

      

        
        
      

      

        
        
No background - the dial stays black.

      

      
Any image works; it is cropped square and resized to 240×240 in
        the browser, exactly like an album photo. A satellite tile of your area turns
        the dial into a live map. Saved Locations keep their own image. Disable
        switches back to the plain dial without touching that stored image - loading
        the location brings its map back. To delete a location's map for good, use
        the Map ✕ button on its row below.

    


    

      
Saved Locations

      

        
        
        
      

      

      
Load applies a place and puts its name in the box, so
        adjusting it and pressing Save updates that place instead of asking for a
        new name. Update on a row overwrites it with the settings above
        without typing anything.

      
Keeps the position, range, screen direction and altitude floor
        above under a name, stored on the device.
        Up to 6.

    


    

      
      
    

    
    
    
Turn the dial to match how the device is sitting, and what is
      ahead of you in the room is ahead of you on screen. The N marker moves round
      the ring to wherever true north has ended up. 0 north, 90 east, 180 south,
      270 west.

    
One sweep of the screen takes exactly one poll, so the picture is
      repainted as the new positions arrive. Under 5 seconds the feed starts refusing.


    
    
ADS-B carries no route, so the destination is fetched by callsign -
      one extra request, and only the first time a flight is seen. The answer is kept
      while the aircraft stays around. Turn this off to keep the sweep running clean.

Forecast Places

      

        
        
        
        
      

      

      
The forecast screen shows the one marked IN USE, and its
        title carries the name - about eight Hangul syllables fit before the
        panel trims it. Coordinates are decimal degrees: 37.6437, 126.7879 is
        백석동. Up to 4, and at least one has to remain. Adding a name that is
        already listed moves it rather than making a second copy.

    


    

      
WiFi Profiles

      

        
        
        
      

      

      
Fallback networks, tried in this order at boot when the one
        above cannot be reached - so the device can be carried between places and
        just plugged in. The guidance screen and recovery AP only appear if every
        profile fails. Up to 5. Passwords are stored on the device and never
        shown here; leave the field blank to keep a saved one.

Recovery Access Point

      
Checking...

      
There is nothing to set. SDP-Recovery comes up by
        itself whenever the device cannot reach your WiFi - at boot, or after about
        twelve seconds if the connection drops while running - and the screen then
        shows the network name, the address to open and what to change. It goes back
        off as soon as WiFi returns. The network is open, and everything behind it
        answers without a password, which is why it is not on the air the rest of
        the time.
